Hello, JOSHIM AHMMED DIGANTA

Professional Video Editor & Broadcast Journalist

I am Joshim Ahmed Diganta, and I live in Dhaka, the capital of Bangladesh. I work as a video editor at Nagorik Television. Let me start by telling you the story of my life. I was born on June 22, 1996. My birth was normal, just like that of any other healthy child. When I was three years old, I contracted polio and became physically disabled. My mother and I were hospitalized for nearly two years during my treatment. Now, I can only walk with great difficulty, relying on my feet. At that time, no school or madrasa would accept me because of my disability. After much effort from my family, I was admitted to a madrasa at the age of six, but I only studied there for two years.
Then began the struggle to get admitted to another school. No one would take me in because I was disabled. Finally, an NGO school called "Dhaka Ahsania Mission" accepted me, and I was the only disabled student there. I made many friends at the school. Since it was hard for me to walk on one foot, I would hold onto my friends' shoulders while walking. During my time at school, older brothers and sisters formed a cultural organization called "Shobuj Patar Arale," where I learned to draw. Whenever there was a drawing competition, my school took me there with great enthusiasm. As it was an NGO school, I also participated in activities related to children's rights. During “Children's Rights Week," our school took us to the National Parliament House, where I met Bipul Bhai from CSID. He informed me that CSID works for the development of disabled children and invited me to join their organization. I joined them, and there was a group of disabled children at CSID called the "Advocacy Group." I was thrilled to be part of the "Advocacy Group" because it was my first experience stepping outside my comfort zone.
At CSID, I made friends with many older brothers and sisters, including Alam Bhai, Robi Bhai, Iftikhar Bhai, Bipul Bhai, Akhtar Bhai, Tupu Bhai, Ashiq Bhai, Shiuli Apa, and Asma Apa, who treated me very kindly. I learned a lot from them and shared all my thoughts with them. There were also some older brothers, sisters, friends, and younger brothers in the Advocacy Group, and I still remember them fondly, especially Moniruzzaman Shuvro, Rubel, Ibrahim Bhai, Faisal Bhai, and Zarina Apa. CSID covered all my educational expenses, and since I loved drawing, they provided me with colors, brushes, and paper. Through CSID, I received training in acting, drawing, and taking pictures with a digital camera. We performed street plays in many places, and a stage play I wrote and directed was showcased at an international theater festival.
We took photographs of disabled children ourselves and organized exhibitions in Dhaka and the vast Barishal Division. I had a strong desire to become a computer engineer, and I mentioned this to Bipul Bhai. After that, whenever there was any work related to computers, he would recommend me first and send me. In this way, I was sent as a representative of CSID to "Ichhe Media." Under Save the Children (Sweden-Denmark), there was a children's organization called "Ichhe Media," which published a monthly magazine called "Amader Khobor," funded by Save the Children. Children from all over Bangladesh contributed to the magazine by writing articles, poetry and sending in drawings. The editing, design, publication, and distribution were all managed by the children themselves. I was assigned the task of drawing for that magazine. I began selecting news sent from across the country and worked on computer printing. Through Rafiq Bhai, the editor at that time, I gradually gained proficiency in all aspects of the work. The magazine had a rule that no one could become the editor directly; the editor was elected from the assistant editors, and their term was one year. I initially served as an assistant editor and later took on the role of editor.
After assuming editorial responsibility, I arranged advanced training for child journalists through a senior journalist from a national newspaper. To ensure children from all over Bangladesh could participate, I sent out several "Amader Khobor" letterboxes in and around Dhaka and provided them with ideas for writing reports. The representatives responsible for "Amader Khobor" collected the news sent by child journalists at designated times. Since "Amader Khobor" did not have permission from the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting of the Bangladesh government, we were unable to distribute it like national newspapers. To effectively manage the magazine and work for the welfare of child journalists, I was given the responsibility for two years instead of the initial one-year term.
Meanwhile, I completed my secondary education and enrolled in higher secondary school. There, I made many friends, including Saimon, Sajib, and Sujon. I still didn't know how to use a walking aid, so I relied on my friends' shoulders to walk. Gradually, I learned to use the walking aid, and after a lot of practice, I can walk now. Previously, I would walk using one hand on my feet and the other on my friends' shoulders. While working at the magazine in 2008, I got the opportunity to work at a national television channel called "Ekushey Television" in the program "Muktokhobor." I worked there for a while, and then I participated as a representative in an international children's film festival through CSID. There, many prominent directors, writers, and luminaries from Bangladesh provided training on film production. The following year, with the support of CSID, I participated in that international children's film festival as a child director, creating a short film. Although the short film did not win any awards, it received much praise because no one had previously made such a short film on the lives of disabled individuals in Bangladesh.
Later, Save the Children announced that they would provide a three-month training course in video editing and operating video cameras. At that time, CSID sent the names of three disabled children, including mine, for training. Ultimately, I was the only one from CSID who remained in training; the other two could not keep up. Being physically disabled, it was challenging for me to handle heavy equipment and run around, so I chose video editing training instead. For video editing training, a well-known media house called "Sublime Media" was chosen, and senior video editor Shahnewaz Sajib Bhai from the private television channel "RTV" was assigned to provide the training. After three months of training, I worked as an Assistant video editor at Sublime Media for nearly four years. At Sublime Media, I worked on many single dramas, serial dramas, television programs, music videos, and documentaries. During the training, I became very good friends with Ramjan Ali. Together, we worked on Ashiqur Rahman's film "Kistimaat." Additionally, I worked individually on Shahin Sumon's film "Sedin Brishti Chhilo."
Then began my journey at a television channel. I worked in the broadcast department at a private television channel, "Mohona Television Ltd." as an "Executive Presentation and Compilation" for almost three years. After that, I have been working as a video editor at "Nagorik Television" since 2018. I am the first disabled person to hold a job at a television channel in Bangladesh. I am very grateful to the management of Nagorik Television for believing in me and providing me with work opportunities, despite the difficulties I might face as a disabled person in coming to the office and working. All members of the Nagorik family are very warm and friendly towards me, especially the owner, Rubana Haque Madam, and department head, Kamruzzaman Babu Bhai. In 2023, through CSID, I had the opportunity to participate in the "10th International Abilympics France 2023" held in Metz City, France. This was my first time participating in an international competition outside Bangladesh. Participants from about 44 countries took part, competing in various categories across 48 subjects. We had 10 participants from Bangladesh, of which I was one of the competitors. I won 3rd place in the ICT category for publishing. During our trip to France, Rafi Bhai and Akhtar Bhai were with us, constantly looking after us.
After returning to Bangladesh, I took a course in digital marketing and website design and development from the Bangladesh Computer Council through CSID. The website you are currently viewing has been completely designed and developed by me. I have a desire to establish my own digital firm where I will work on video editing, graphic design, digital marketing, and website design and development. I will continue my journalism work and occasionally create short films.

Thank you for your valuable time.

Joshim Ahmed Diganta
Video Editor
Nagorik Television

Typoparty is a a cultural event made to highlight typography, calligraphy, font design, motion design and interactive design. Typoparty International Festival 2023 will take place in Metz, offering several workshops, conferences and exhibitions on the 26th of May to the 3rd of June 2023 at Metz Museum Center. For this first edition the festival will not have only one identity, the typography being a varied art, the organizing committee wishes to represent this using a set of visual identities. Upon this test project, we ask you to create one of the visual identities of Typoparty Festival 2023.
Task 1 - Poster & Wordmark, This task must be completed in less than 4 hours on the first day.
Task 2 - Ticket, You must design a ticket that will be given at the entrance of a workshop. The ticket will include a detachable part, there will be then 2 parts: Part 1 and Part 2.

The ticket number and the logo must be included on the 2 parts.
Task 3 - Facebook Post, A Facebook campaign will be set up during the event, we ask you to design a template for declinable publications, from famous quotes.
Day - 1 (March 23rd): On the day before the competition, contestants will be welcomed on the stand by the members of the jury. A briefing about the organization of the contest and about the security rules will be arranged. Each contestant will receive a schedule with the detailed working hours. Contestants will draw lots to be assigned to a work station, where they may drop off their equipment. Day 1 (March 24th): Contestants will have 4 hours to work on their task assignment. Contestants must finish task 1 on this day. Day 2 (March 25th): Contestants will have 2 hours to complete task 2 and task 3.
